**The standing rule for every item here (learned three times this week):** first decide **slow vs broken vs meaningless-remotely** by reproducing in isolation — never widen a timeout to make a red go away (the branch-mode "timeout" was a real product bug; a widen would have buried it), and never skip without a named reason and a tracked issue.

## R1 — branch-mode Code tab: a base ref that exists reads as missing *(the known one — #1701)*

Open a branch-mode diff over the bind and the tree never hydrates: `getStatus` returns `BASE_BRANCH_NOT_FOUND` for `origin/master` even though the ref **provably exists** on the remote box (verified against the live failing repo — all three ref lookups resolve). Suspected mechanism (labeled hypothesis in the issue): the status subscription fires early, correctly errors before the clone lands the ref, and the git-refs watcher never re-fires it over the bind — the third member of the **watcher-over-remote genus** (see R5). 9 scenarios carry counted `REMOTE-SKIP-1701` markers; they un-skip the day #1701's fix lands. **Destination: its own repro-first PR.**

## R2 — the timeout cluster: ~21 scenarios, slow-vs-broken UNDECIDED

Code-tab (12) and canvas (9) scenarios expire even at the remote-widened waits — including 180-second hydration ceilings. **A 180s wait expiring is not "a bit slow"; it is the signature of something that never arrives** — exactly how R1 looked before diagnosis. Each of these needs the bounded one-scenario treatment R1 got: run alone over the bind, watch the wire (does the response arrive late, or never?), then bin it — *genuinely slow* → a scenario-scoped wait with the measured number recorded; *never arrives* → a product bug filed like #1701. Expect this cluster to hide at least one more real bug. **Destination: diagnosis first; no widening without numbers.**

## R3 — scenarios that are meaningless on a remote bind: the local-daemon-lifecycle class

`kaval-daemon.feature` (5 scenarios) asserts things like "kill the local kaval and watch kolu react" — but over the bind, the daemons live on the remote box; there is no local kaval, and the scenario's premise dissolves. Same genus as the (already-shipped) inventory gap: observing **this** machine vs the **bound** machine. These are legitimate skips *only after* each gets its remote story named: does a remote equivalent exist (the `hostInventory` member #1686 shipped covers observation — do the lifecycle assertions have a remote counterpart worth writing?), or is the scenario local-only by nature (then a permanent, reasoned skip)? **Destination: a product decision per scenario, then named counted skips.**

## R4 — terminal-creation races: "created but no new id" (~3 scenarios)

Create a terminal over the bind and the id sometimes never shows up in the assertion window. Could be an ordinary flake (these features have local flake history) or a real remote race in create→collection propagation. **Destination: reproduce once in isolation over the bind — remote-reproducible → a bug; local-too → it belongs to the flaky-debt ledger entry (L10), not here.**

## R5 — the watcher-over-remote genus *(the meta-finding — worth more than any single fix)*

Three separate incidents this week share one shape — *a watcher that works locally misbehaves once the daemon is remote or long-lived*: **#1680** (codex WAL `fs.watch` dies on darwin, agent state freezes), **#1691 → fixed by #1692** (the parcel watchman-probe leaked a zombie per subscribe), and now **#1701** (the git-refs watcher not re-firing status). Three is a pattern: our file/ref watchers were all written under a locality assumption the remote bind breaks. This entry is the audit: enumerate every watcher in the padi stack (agent-state sensors · git refs · repo pulses · session files), and for each, answer *what happens when its event is late, lost, or on another machine* — before the fourth incident finds us. **Destination: one audit pass, producing either fixes or per-watcher liveness notes; #1680's fix (the reconcile-poll backstop) is the template.**
