The client half: the code that spawns, watches, recycles and upgrades a daemon.
It runs in the *client* process, never the daemon, and carries no `@kolu/*` app
dependency. Its only workspace edges are the daemon half and `@kolu/surface`,
which it dials the frozen control core through.

## The face is Effect-native

Every verb on this page returns an `Effect`, and so does every plug it takes
from a consumer: `connect`, `probe`, `driver.spawn`, `RestartSteps`' three
steps, `drain` and `awaitExit`. There is no Promise-shaped twin, so a boot that
loses a race, a restart you abandoned and a drain past its ceiling each tear
their own work down.

Three seams are synchronous facts rather than operations:

| Seam | Why it is not an `Effect` |
| --- | --- |
| `Endpoint.current()` | A read of held state, whose eager throw callers rely on. |
| `DaemonConnection`'s `dispose()` | The supervisor tears down from paths that cannot await. |
| `DaemonConnection`'s `onClose(cb)` | The same: it is called from paths that cannot await. |

Nothing on `EndpointSpec` is Promise-shaped.

- `readProcessIdentity`, `readSocketHolders` and `readProcessState` each state
  `Effect.Effect<…, OsfactsClientError>`.
- The error channel is the union of **`osfacts-client`**, the package that
  satisfies those seams and takes `effect` as its one runtime dependency. Their
  success halves (`SocketOccupancy`, `SocketHolder`) come from there too.
- An identity read that fails is a typed failure the endpoint answers (report
  `dead`, then propagate), not a defect that strands the UI at `connecting`.
- `awaitExit` takes no `AbortSignal` and no `AbortController`. Fiber
  interruption tells a poll-based exit oracle to stop once the drain ceiling has
  won, and interruption cannot be refused.
- Why: [Why the supervisor is a separate package](/surface/the-daemon-spine#why-the-supervisor-is-a-separate-package).

## The dial leaf — `/dial`

`@kolu/surface-daemon-supervisor/dial` carries exactly three things:
`dialSocket` (the unix-socket connect/error race, won once),
`DaemonContractSkewError` plus its brand-checked `isContractSkewError`, and the
`DaemonConnection` shape a handshake produces.

| entry | carries | what it does not compile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `@kolu/surface-daemon-supervisor/dial` | `dialSocket`, `DaemonContractSkewError`, `isContractSkewError`, `DaemonConnection` | `endpoint.ts` — drivers, convergence, `osfacts-client` |

- `@kolu/padi-client/dial` needs those three and nothing more, and it is the
  entry an out-of-repo consumer hydrates on its own. What that consumer reaches,
  its `tsc` compiles.
- Reaching them through the barrel instead compiles `endpoint.ts`: the drivers,
  the convergence probe, and through the process-identity seam
  `osfacts-client`, a second npins pin to graft and guard.
- `endpoint.ts` imports the three from the leaf and re-exports them, so the
  barrel's surface is unchanged. Its whole compile cost is `node:net` and
  `effect`.
- `@kolu/padi-client`'s hydrate guard asserts that no bare daemon barrel is in
  its import closure.
- The copy/compile split stands: a consumer still copies the supervisor's
  directory, whose manifest still names `osfacts-client`.

### `createEndpoint`

Builds the state machine and returns an unforgeable public `Endpoint`.

`createEndpoint({ home, readProcessIdentity, readSocketHolders, readProcessState, policy, probe, connect: (socketPath) => …, … })`

<Snippet src="packages/surface/example/snippets/supervisor.ts" region="endpoint" />

| Option | What you pass |
| --- | --- |
| `home` | `DaemonHomePaths`, the same spine primitive `daemonMain` takes. Gate and socket come from it. |
| `readProcessIdentity` | Start-qualified gate ownership, as a `ReadProcessIdentityAsync`. |
| `readSocketHolders` | Gate-less squatter recovery, as a `ReadSocketHolders`. |
| `readProcessState` | Whether the holder is stopped, so its graceful reap leg is skippable. A `ReadProcessState`. |
| `policy` | Your whole convergence surface: who I am, and how I converge. |
| `probe` | The convergence probe, usually [`probeDaemonIdentity`](#probedaemonidentityoptssocketpath). |
| `connect` | Receives the socket path from the framework, so callers never re-thread it. |

- All three OS-fact readers are required, never defaulted, because the endpoint
  performs no platform traversal of its own.
- `readProcessIdentity` is `(pid: number) => Effect.Effect<ProcessIdentity | undefined, OsfactsClientError>`.
  It runs off the serving loop, so an osfacts spawn never blocks it. Prefer
  `processIdentityAsync(bin)`, with `bin` resolved once at the composition root.
- The awaitable type lives in this package beside `EndpointSpec`. The daemon
  half keeps only the sync `ReadProcessIdentity`, which the synchronous
  gate-claim paths use.
- The boot trio lives in a package `WeakMap`, not on the returned object.

#### An unreadable gate

The endpoint's holder probe folds the daemon half's three-way gate-read law
exhaustively. An unreadable gate (EACCES or EIO) is not free and not stale.

- When `readGateIdentity` yields `unreadable`, `ensure` and recycle throw before
  any unlink, kill or spawn.
- Status still emits `connecting → dead`, then rethrows, so the UI never wedges.
- A mode-`000` gate is the unit pin (`recycleForeignGate.test.ts`).

#### The `incompatible` state

`incompatible` is the proven contract-skew verdict: a respawn from the realised
closure already skewed, or a refuse-policy survivor skews at handshake.

- It is reported with both contract versions on the status
  (`{ state: "incompatible", daemonVersion, requiredVersion }`).
- It is never collapsed into `dead`/`degraded`. A consumer deriving affordances
  from the state sum must not offer a restart against it, because restarting
  cannot fix a skew. Only changing the closure can.
- A restart hold does not coerce it. A skew inside a restart is that restart's
  terminal verdict, not a transition.

### `Endpoint`

The handle `createEndpoint` returns, generic over the client `C` and the
identity `I`.

- Its states are `connecting → connected | dead | incompatible`, then
  `connected → degraded` if the daemon dies mid-session.
- The public verbs are [`converge(endpoint)`](#convergeendpoint) and
  [`recycle(endpoint, steps)`](#recycleendpoint-steps--serializerestartendpoint).
- The boot-method trio (`ensure` / `adoptOrEnsure` / `adoptOrSpawnOrRefuse`) is
  absent from the public `Endpoint` type and from the runtime object.
- Private binds live only in a package `WeakMap` keyed by the genuine
  `createEndpoint` handle. Kit modules (`converge`, `recycle`) and same-package
  tests resolve them through a package-internal relative import of
  `endpoint.private.ts`. There is no public `asEndpointInternal` and no
  `./testing` subpath.
- `current()` is the live connection. `holdRestarting` is the recycle
  emit-guard.

### `destructiveRecycleSteps()`

Named no-preservation steps for a fail-closed recycle. All steps are still
required.

### `survivableSpawnDriver(cfg, deps?)`

The default `DaemonDriver`: `systemd-run --user` under a service, and a
detached `+unref` child otherwise.

- `DaemonDriver.spawn` is an `Effect<void>` value, not a method. Re-running it
  is "spawn again".

#### `cfg.env` per launch mode

`survivableSpawnDriver`'s `cfg.env` is read differently by the mode the driver
picks, so compose it for the mode you will hit.

| Mode | How `env` is read |
| --- | --- |
| Detached (the production default) | `env` is the complete child env. Nothing from the parent is layered under it, so a partial set spawns a daemon with no `PATH` or `HOME`. |
| Under systemd | `env` overlays the unit's PAM/manager env via `--setenv`, so a partial set is fine. |

- On the detached path, carry the daemon's whole base. Compose it from a fixed
  allowlist, as the snippet above does. kolu mines `kolu-pty`'s
  `SPAWN_ENV_ALLOWLIST`.
- Launch mode and env inheritance are one union field, so the illegal
  combination is unspellable. **`fromSource`** omitted (or `false`) is a normal
  launch: systemd-eligible, with `env` complete.
- An object `{ inheritParentEnv }` forces the detached branch even under a
  session, for an actual source launch or a built binary a box forces detached.
- `inheritParentEnv` decides whether `env` layers over the supervisor's full
  parent env. Use `true` only for an actual from-source launch (the dev
  nix-shell env). A built forced-detached daemon uses
  `{ inheritParentEnv: false }` and gets `env` alone.
- Why: [Why the framework takes values, not loose fields](/surface/the-daemon-spine#why-the-framework-takes-values-not-loose-fields).

### `scrubDaemonNodeOptions(nodeOptions)`

Strips dev-only Node flags from an inherited `NODE_OPTIONS`, so a spawned
daemon never opens the supervisor's inspector or writes its profiles.

- It returns `undefined` when nothing of value remains, so the variable is
  dropped rather than emptied.
- It is opt-in per driver.

### `recycle(endpoint, steps)` · `serializeRestart(endpoint)`

`recycle` is the composed `capture → drain → recycle → reattach` sequence, as
one `Effect`. `serializeRestart` returns a single-flight `triggerRestart`.

- `RestartSteps`' three members are `Effect`s too. `capture` is a value,
  because an effect is already the description of work not yet done.
- `triggerRestart` coalesces rather than queues: a second caller joins the
  restart already in flight and gets its outcome, instead of waiting and then
  running a second one.

### `waitForPidGone(pid, opts?)`

An `Effect<boolean>` that polls `isHolderLive` until the pid is reaped
(`ESRCH`), so a respawn never races a live gate holder.

### `reapHolder(pid, readProcessState, opts?)`

An `Effect<ReapOutcome, OsfactsClientError>`: the two-deadline stop every
supervisor kill site shares.

`SIGTERM` → wait `REAP_TERM_CEILING_MS` (120 s) → `SIGKILL` → wait
`REAP_KILL_CEILING_MS` (5 s).

- `readProcessState` is required, as the injected OS fact, never a default.
- A holder observed stopped (`T`/`t`) skips the graceful leg, because a stopped
  process cannot run a shutdown handler. `SIGKILL` lands at once.
- A read that cannot complete travels the error channel, where the endpoint
  reports `dead` and rethrows. It is never a silent "not stopped".
- It answers `{ kind: "reaped", endedBy, waitedMs, reason? }` or
  `{ kind: "survived", waitedMs, reason? }`. An operator reads which signal
  ended it, how long it took, and whether the graceful window was skipped.

### `ReadSocketHolders`

The type of the `readSocketHolders` inject:
`(socketPath: string) => Effect.Effect<SocketOccupancy, OsfactsClientError>`.

- The reader that satisfies it is `osfactsSocketHolders(bin)` from
  **`osfacts-client`**, bound to an already-resolved osfacts binary path
  (`bakedOsFactsBin(<your env var>)`). This package never learns which variable
  a consumer bakes.
- It calls `osfacts socket-holders <path> --procs`. The platform split, the
  parse, the honesty policy and the fold live in the tool and its client, not
  here.
- Bind it to the same resolved path as `processIdentityAsync`, so one root
  spells its environment variable once for all the OS facts.

### `ReadProcessState`

The type of the `readProcessState` inject:
`(pid: number) => Effect.Effect<ProcessState | undefined, OsfactsClientError>`.

- The reader that satisfies it is `processStateAsync(bin)` from
  **`osfacts-client`**, bound to the same already-resolved osfacts binary path
  (`osfacts snapshot --pids <pid> --status`).
- `undefined` is the honest dead-pid answer.
- The `T`/`t` ⇒ stopped fold lives once in this package, not in the reader.

### `SocketOccupancy` · `SocketHolder`

The three-way answer to who holds a socket, never one possibly-empty list.

| Answer | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| `{ kind: "held", holders }` | `holders` is a non-empty tuple by type (`readonly [SocketHolder, ...SocketHolder[]]`), so a consumer never writes an unreachable empty-check. |
| `{ kind: "none" }` | Proven nothing holds it. Only linux's world-readable `/proc/net/unix` can prove this. |
| `{ kind: "unattributed", detail }` | Something may hold it and the OS would not say what: a bound-but-unreadable holder, or a search that could not complete. |

- Only `none` is evidence of freedom, and the endpoint still re-probes before
  spawning.
- Both types are re-exported from `osfacts-client`, whose
  `foldSocketOccupancy(reading)` produces them from a `SocketHoldersReading`.
  One fold, so kolu and drishti cannot spell the three-way differently.

### `SocketSquatterForeignError` · `isSocketSquatterForeignError(err)`

Thrown when the process holding the rendezvous socket did not complete the
kaval handshake. Such a foreign or non-conforming holder is never killed.

- It carries `socketPath`, `holders: SocketHolder[]` and `detail?: string`, so
  the refusal names the squatter.
- Each holder is a pid and an optional `command`. The command is absent, never
  `"?"`, when the identity read lost the race, so a sentinel cannot be mistaken
  for a process actually named `?`.
- When the OS named nobody, `detail` is the `unattributed` reading's own words
  (`the holder search could not complete (darwin_proc_fds: BLIND_OR_EMPTY)`),
  which the message renders instead of "an unidentifiable process".
- On darwin the nameless case is the ordinary one, so the explanation, not just
  the decision, has to survive the fold.
- The guard is brand-checked and realm-robust.

### `SocketProbeIndeterminateError` · `isSocketProbeIndeterminateError(err)`

Thrown when a one-shot socket connect probe times out (`SocketServeState`
`"indeterminate"`). It proves neither free nor dead.

- The endpoint never kills, unlinks or spawns on it.
- It carries `gatePath`, `socketPath` and an optional `gatePid`.
- Failures after `connecting` emit `dead`, then rethrow, so status never
  wedges.

### `DaemonContractSkewError` · `isContractSkewError(err)`

The one typed connect rejection that proves incompatibility. It is also exported
from [`/dial`](#the-dial-leaf--dial).

- It is constructed from fields (`{ subject, daemonVersion, requiredVersion, pid? }`),
  with the message derived from them, so no consumer re-parses the prose.
- `readonly daemonVersion` and `requiredVersion` ride the instance, so
  downstream translators (a typed RPC rethrow, a status arm) read them
  structurally.
- The optional `readonly pid` is the skewed daemon's self-reported OS pid. The
  gate-less-squatter recovery of an old orphan throws here before a connection
  exists, and this is its identity attestation.
- The guard is brand-checked (realm-robust), never `instanceof`.

### `ENDPOINT_STATES` · `ENDPOINT_STATE_DOWN` · `isDownEndpointState(s)`

The state tuple and its total down/terminal classification. Both live in the
zero-dependency, browser-safe `/states` subpath, and are re-exported from the
package root.

- `ENDPOINT_STATE_DOWN` is a `Record<EndpointState, boolean>`, so a new state is
  a compile error at the home until it is classified.
- Consumers (a dial-ended check, a client presentation table) read
  `isDownEndpointState` instead of hand-spelling the
  `degraded || dead || incompatible` disjunct.

## The convergence kit

`converge(endpoint)` is the only boot verb. It probes the running daemon's
identity, asks a pure `decide`, admits drain through the per-boot budget when
the daemon is drainable, and enacts through the endpoint's private boot methods.

[kaval](/kaval) declares `recycle-on-skew`. [padi](/padi) declares
`drain-newer-else-refuse` with a drain-capable handshake. The step-by-step for
both is in
[How to recycle and upgrade a daemon](/surface/recycle-and-upgrade), and the
rules the kit enforces are in
[Daemon invariants](/surface/surface-daemon-invariants).

### `converge(endpoint)`

A single fold over every observation, then decide, then a budget-gated drain,
then a private bind. It takes a genuine `createEndpoint` handle only.

- It returns a typed `ConvergenceOutcome` whose kinds either always carry their
  anomaly or never do. There are no optionals.
- Anomaly arms carry typed evidence as data: `running` and `expected`
  identities, `drained` and `observed` instance keys. `detail` is human garnish
  only.
- Same-lineage admitted drains continue (F1a).
- A null re-probe is `identity-unverifiable` (F1b).
- Give-up re-decides characterization without re-draining.
- Connector arms (ssh) use [`convergeAdmit`](#convergeadmitopts) with the same
  policy object.

#### Single observation authority

Every observed identity flows through one internal fold (`foldObserved`): the
initial probe, bind characterization, the post-drain successor, the post-give-up
bind, and the drainable re-probe.

- Every bind transition (plain, recycle, post-drain, give-up) flows through one
  `BindResult` consumer (`consumeBindResult`). Call sites never inspect
  `r.kind`.
- The fold always runs `decide(policy, identity | null)`, never a hand-copied
  compat/match subset.
- It owns connection lifecycle, so refused, not-adopted and probe-failed exits
  call the idempotent `releaseHeld` unconditionally. There is no `holding`
  mirror.
- It preserves four-valued characterizations
  (`characterized | absent | failed | uncorrelated`).
- It reports `unconverged.running: null` when the running identity is honestly
  unknown, and never fabricates `expected`.
- A characterization probe throw is `probe-failed` with the true message, never
  a catch-to-null into `identity-unverifiable`.
- Named probe instance keys must match `conn.startedAt`. A mismatch is
  `uncorrelated`.

### `convergeAdmit(opts)`

The connector arm, an `Effect<ConvergeAdmitVerdict>`. It takes
`{ running, budget, drain, awaitExit, … }`.

- `budget` is a `ConnectorDrainBudget`, which makes recycle and nudge-human
  unspellable.
- `drain` and `awaitExit` are `Effect`s.
- `awaitExit` succeeds only on process exit, never on link loss (F3).

### `probeDaemonIdentity(opts)(socketPath)`

The endpoint probe for the frozen fragment, as a full `ConvergenceProbe`. It
takes `{ capability, drainCeilingMs? }`.

- It answers `null` only for an honest `ECONNREFUSED` or `ENOENT`.
- Every other dial or hello failure throws, including a control-core version
  other than the frozen `1.0`, and an unspeakable peer
  (`UnspeakableProtocolError`).
- It throws when a live pre-fragment daemon answers the socket but has no
  `core.hello` route.

A consumer with a named upgrade window may catch only that structured
route-missing failure and project it as an older-build observation. Kaval does
this, so its not-drainable policy reports the existing human nudge.

- Such a consumer never turns the served-daemon fact into `null`, and catches no
  other probe error.
- The narrowing derives on the Effect RPC failure. The oRPC `NOT_FOUND` frame
  does not exist on this wire.
- A peer from a previous epoch is a different observation. See
  [The third observation](#the-third-observation--unspeakable-protocol).

### `probeDaemonIdentityFrom(opts)`

The single probe-assembly authority for an already-dialled connector.

- `probeDaemonIdentityFrom({ client, dispose, capability: "not-drainable" })`
  omits the exit-oracle fields.
- `probeDaemonIdentityFrom({ client, dispose, capability: "drainable", awaitExit, drainCeilingMs })`
  requires both of them.
- ssh keeps its stronger process-exit oracle instead of treating link loss as
  exit.

### `readControlCoreHello(client)` · `ControlCoreProbeClient`

Reads and validates the frozen hello on its one baked 30 s deadline: the
frozen-version check, plus the joint `buildId`/`commit` fact.

- `ControlCoreProbeClient` is the already-dialled client shape it takes:
  `client.surface.control.core.hello()`, which is itself an `Effect`.

### `dialSocket(socketPath)`

The package's own unix-socket dial, as an `Effect<Socket>`, so the control-core
leg owns its framing tap.

- A dial interrupted before it settles destroys the half-open socket rather than
  abandoning it.

### `isNoListenerError(error)`

The one `ECONNREFUSED`/`ENOENT` classifier shared by generic and pre-fragment
daemon probes.

### `decide(policy, running)`

The pure fold, and the sole decision authority under `converge`. It reads
`policy.baked`; there is no free-standing baked argument.

### `createDrainBudget` · `createConnectorDrainBudget` · `outcomeAdopted` · `outcomeAnomaly`

Opaque budget handles and outcome constructors. There are no public admit
can-openers, and the connector mint is type-gated.

### `InstanceKey` · `instanceKeyFromStartedAt` · `instanceKeyTag`

A named instance, or `pre-instance` when `startedAt` is absent, which means
older. The absence is never overloaded onto null.

### `BindResult` · `BoundResidentCharacterization`

The private-bind result. Adopted residents carry a four-valued characterization:
`characterized | absent | failed | uncorrelated`.

### `drainAndAwaitExit(drain, awaitExit, { ceilingMs })`

The framework-run drain-and-confirm-exit skeleton both enactments share. Its
signature is
`drainAndAwaitExit(drain: Effect<void>, awaitExit: Effect<void>, { ceilingMs })`.

- Both plugs are forked into the primitive's own scope, so the ceiling winning
  interrupts the exit oracle.
- `awaitExit`'s error channel is `never` by type, which states the F3 contract
  in the signature rather than in a comment.

### `ConvergenceAnomaly`

`adopted-stale | skew-refused | unconverged | cross-supervisor`. Each arm
carries typed evidence.

- `unconverged.running` is nullable, for an unknown identity.
- `link-failed` is session-owned.

### `UnconvergedCause`

`budget-exhausted | drain-not-taken | adopt-bind-failed | identity-unverifiable | probe-failed | unspeakable-protocol`,
with the evidence as data under `unconverged`.

- The `unspeakable-protocol` arm carries `socketPath`, `gatePath` and the pid
  that was classified.
- It rides a refusal only when the gate stopped naming that pid before the
  takeover could act.

### `UnspeakableEvidence` · `unspeakableClause(evidence)`

Why a peer is unspeakable, as data: `{ trigger: "undecodable-frame", frame }` or
`{ trigger: "silence", silentForMs }`.

- There is no third arm.
- `unspeakableClause` is shared, so the dial path, the corroboration site and
  the fold cannot drift in what they tell an operator.

### `UnspeakableProtocolError` · `isUnspeakableProtocolError(err)`

The transport fact, raised by `probeDaemonIdentity`'s dial at one of the two
triggers. It is never raised by the hello deadline, a close, or a member error.

- It carries `socketPath` and the `evidence` field.
- Uncorroborated, it is an ordinary `probe-failed`.
- The guard is brand-checked and realm-robust.

### `UnspeakablePeerError` · `isUnspeakablePeerError(err)`

The corroborated verdict: the same fact plus the two attestations the endpoint
adds. The gate at this rendezvous is ours, and its `pid` passed the holder
identity law.

- It carries `gatePath` and `pid` beside the transport fields.
- Only this becomes the `unspeakable-protocol` observation.

### `UNSPEAKABLE_SILENCE_MS`

The silence bound, 8 s. It is pinned between two facts of the link.

- The link pings every 5 s, and a peer of this epoch pongs *below* its handlers,
  so a merely slow daemon has still demonstrably spoken.
- The link's keep-alive backstop ends a silent connection only past 60 s, after
  which there is nothing left to classify.

### `contractIsNewer` · `contractIsCompatible` · `buildsMatch`

Re-exported from `@kolu/surface-daemon`. Contract versions are ordered; build
ids are match-only.

### The third observation — `unspeakable-protocol`

A daemon from a **previous protocol epoch** cannot be asked its version at all,
because version negotiation happens inside the protocol that was replaced.
Convergence therefore has a third narrowly-typed peer observation beside
"identity" and "absent".

Such a peer betrays itself in exactly two ways, and which one you get depends on
who greets whom:

| Trigger | What happens | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `undecodable-frame` | The peer speaks first, in a framing we cannot parse. | A bounded, JSON-quoted `frame` excerpt. |
| `silence` | The peer waits for a greeting in a protocol we do not speak: it accepts the connection, takes our frames, and answers nothing. | `silentForMs`, the bound it stayed mute through. |

- The `silence` arm is measured against the previous release: its oRPC
  `ServerPeer` waits for its own client hello, our ndjson frames never look like
  one, and the connection dies by timeout with no first frame to fail decoding.
- Both triggers are the same verdict, so they are one error type with the
  trigger as a tagged field, never two classes a consumer would have to union.
- The fact is raised under those triggers alone. The observation additionally
  requires two attestations: the gate file at this rendezvous is ours, and its
  pid passed the holder identity law.
- An uncorroborated trigger stays `probe-failed`, and a foreign socket-squatter
  keeps the `SocketSquatterForeignError` path. A merely slow peer and a stranger
  on our socket both stop at the transport fact.
- Classification happens at the trigger, not by waiting out the frozen hello's
  30 s deadline. That keeps `awaitHelloGone` responsive inside a drain ceiling
  when the peer will never answer.

#### The takeover

The disposition is takeover, for every policy. The contract-skew policy is not
consulted, because an undecodable wire is not a skew and the drain verb does not
exist on it. What is available is the act the drain verb requests: the daemon's
own in-process shutdown, asked for with a signal instead of a message.

| step | what happens |
| --- | --- |
| re-attest | The gate must still name the exact pid that was classified. If it names anyone else, nothing is signalled and the pass refuses with the `unspeakable-protocol` cause. |
| `SIGTERM` | The old daemon's own shutdown runs (persist, close, release the gate), bounded by `REAP_TERM_CEILING_MS` (120 s). |
| `SIGKILL` | Only past the graceful deadline, bounded by `REAP_KILL_CEILING_MS` (5 s). Still alive after it, and the endpoint reports `dead` and fails loud. |
| spawn | A daemon of this epoch is started and connected at the same rendezvous. It seeds from disk, so loss is bounded by the old daemon's autosave debounce. |

- The graceful leg is skipped entirely when the holder is observed stopped
  (`T`/`t`): a stopped process cannot run a shutdown handler, because the signal
  is queued, not delivered.
- On that skipped path `SIGKILL` lands immediately. That is the
  `reason: "stopped"` in the outcome.
- The takeover is logged as one operator-readable line: what was stopped, why it
  was provably ours, which signal ended it, how long the wait took, and whether
  the graceful leg was skipped.
- The outcome is `recycled`, because the holder was replaced, not adopted.
- Kaval's `recycle` reaches the same act by the same path.
- Why: [Recycling a daemon without lying to its clients](/surface/the-daemon-spine#recycling-a-daemon-without-lying-to-its-clients).
