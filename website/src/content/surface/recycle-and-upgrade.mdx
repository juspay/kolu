When you ship a new [daemon](/surface/glossary) build, an older one may still be
holding the gate with clients attached. `converge()` detects the mismatch,
decides what to do by your declared policy, and enacts it through the endpoint.
The client sees no more than a brief reconnect.

## 1. Declare a policy

A `ConvergencePolicy<Cap>` holds everything a consumer declares about
convergence: who I am (`baked`), how I converge per trigger (contract skew
against same-contract build change), and, when drainable, the Cap-gated
`drainBudget`. Two shapes ship:

- **`recycle-on-skew`** ([kaval](/kaval)): a mismatched daemon is killed and respawned. It has no budget, because a policy that is not drainable cannot spell one.
- **`drain-newer-else-refuse`** (padi): the daemon with the strictly newer contract version wins. It is drained in and replaces the old one; an older or equal contract refuses. The budget survives adopts, so a cross-supervisor fight terminates as `cross-supervisor` rather than looping.

## 2. Converge before you connect

State the policy (and probe) on `createEndpoint`, then call `converge(endpoint)`,
the only boot verb:

<Snippet src="packages/surface/example/snippets/supervisor.ts" region="converge" />

`converge` runs three steps:

1. **probe** the running identity, over the version-agnostic channel, so a skew cannot hide it;
2. **decide** with the pure `decide` fold (no I/O, just the policy table);
3. **enact** through the endpoint's private boot methods, budget-gated when drainable.

It returns a `ConvergenceOutcome` with an optional `ConvergenceAnomaly`. Wire
that to your own status surface and logs. Connector arms use `convergeAdmit`
with the same policy.

`probeDaemonIdentity({ capability: "not-drainable" })(socketPath)` owns a fresh
local socket dial; the drainable form also requires `drainCeilingMs`. A
connector that has already dialled a combined client uses
`probeDaemonIdentityFrom` instead, supplying that client, its disposer, and the
transport's stronger exit oracle. Both forms assemble the same
`ConvergenceProbe`, and the client form never turns a lost link into daemon
absence.

## 3. What a recycle does

When the decision is to recycle, the endpoint:

1. reads the gate under the pid-first law (`readGateIdentity` plus injected `readProcessIdentity`, with a ±2 s start-time match) to see whether a live survivor holds it. A one-field gate falls back to `isHolderLive` and a socket accept.
2. **kills** it, then runs `waitForPidGone`, so the respawn never races a live gate holder.
3. calls `driver.spawn()`, a survivable spawn (`systemd-run --user`, or detached `+unref`).
4. runs `waitForSocket`, then `connect()` and the identity handshake.

A connected client has its daemon replaced underneath, and its session loop
reconnects, re-running `converge`, with an `onStatus(degraded)` bridging the
gap. `recycle(endpoint, steps)` wraps this as
`capture → drain → recycle → reattach`, so a session can be captured and
re-adopted live across the kill.

<Aside type="caution" title="Compare contract versions, not strings">
  The decision keys on ordered contract versions and match-only build ids, never
  on a literal string equality. A newer build with a compatible contract is
  recognized as compatible. See
  [Daemon invariants](/surface/surface-daemon-invariants).
</Aside>
