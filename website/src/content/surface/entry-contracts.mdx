The [client half](/surface/the-client-half) rests on a small wire contract: the shape of one entry's membership and status, and of the frames that carry its calls. Every choice in it applies one principle: the framework owns the keyed volatility, and the app owns policy.

## Why the status is a sum, not a flag

The [surface map](/surface/glossary) publishes each entry's status as `EntryStatus<Failure>`, a sum of exactly three arms:

<Snippet src="packages/surface/example/snippets/map.ts" region="entrystatus" />

There is no `absent` arm: absence means absence. A collection already says "not a member" by not containing the key, and a second encoding of the same fact is a second writer waiting to disagree. So membership lives in the key set, status in the value, and one writer publishes both.

The three arms exist because the client must tell apart states that look alike but need opposite responses:

- **warming**: the system is doing something and will resolve on its own. First provision and a transient reconnect backoff both land here. The right UI is a spinner, not an alarm. Because of `unobservable` (below), this arm means a campaign the publisher is narrating to you right now, so you may put a clock on it.
- **connected**: live. `clockOffset` is this entry's [clock seam](#why-the-clock-is-per-entry).
- **failed**: it will not resolve unless someone acts. It is a bounded give-up, or a standing refusal ("another owner holds this host"). The right UI is an actionable card, not an eternal spinner.

## Why there is a fourth arm on your side

`entry(key).state()` has one more arm that the wire never carries: **unobservable**. The client's liveness floor mints it when your own link to the publisher is dead. It means "we cannot see this entry".

### The bug it prevents

The floor demotes a `connected` entry when your link dies, so nothing renders green over a dead transport. Demoting it to `warming` merged two opposite facts: "the host is coming up" and "we cannot see the publisher at all".

A consumer that only spins is fine either way. A consumer that times the entry can certify a healthy thing dead. Kolu did: a backgrounded tab lost its socket, the entry dropped to `warming`, and a 30-second boot deadline declared a daemon that had run for twelve hours dead ([kolu#2129](https://github.com/juspay/kolu/pull/2129)).

### Why an arm and not a flag

A separate arm makes every `.exhaustive()` match in every consumer say what it does while blind. The compiler asks the question. An optional flag on `warming` would be a field you can ignore, which is a rule you can forget.

### What it carries

- `membershipId`: the floor is about liveness, not identity, so it is still the same member.
- `published: "warming" | "connected"`: the last thing the publisher said. You can show "last seen: connected" without the framework pretending the claim still holds.
- No `clockOffset` and no `connection`. A frozen in-progress word cannot keep narrating work that stopped, and there is nothing green to paint.

`failed` is not floored, because a post-mortem does not go stale the way a live claim does. That is why `published` has no `failed` value.

For a consumer that only wants a spinner, `isSettling(state)` covers both `warming` and `unobservable`. Anything that puts a clock on an entry must narrow the union itself, which is the point.

The arm has no wire schema: it is a projection of your own transport. So `entryStatusSchema` cannot encode it, and a floored value can never be republished.

## Why a failure carries its evidence

The `failed` arm is a **`FailureRecord`**: the reason, and the output that evidences it, as one named value. Neither half is optional, because a cause without what the machine printed is a card nobody can act on. One value is a rule you cannot forget, where two correlated fields are a rule to remember. For the same reason, a `disconnected` session state carries an optional `refuse: FailureRecord`.

The pairing also survives the liveness floor. The floor drops the live `connection` payload when the browser's link goes quiet. If the output tail rode that payload, a laptop waking from sleep would see a reason with no evidence. The floor drops liveness payloads, and a failure record is not one. The `failed` arm carries no `connection` at all, so the old read does not compile.

### Where `warming` ends and `failed` begins

The session's domain failure draws the line.

- A specific failure, such as a contract skew or a cross-supervisor refusal, is a standing condition. It projects `failed`.
- No specific failure means a transient drop. It projects `warming`.

Collapsing the two produces two opposite bugs: a retriable reconnect shown as red "failed" makes a live system look dead, and a standing refusal shown as `warming` spins forever. The sum makes both unrepresentable.

### The domain owns its failures

`Failure` is a type parameter (defaulting to `unknown`), inferred from the map's required `failure` schema. The generic package cannot know a domain's failure taxonomy, so it carries only the discriminant and one thing of its own.

A domain instantiates `EntryStatus<PadiEntryFailure>` at its own map. `PadiEntryFailure` is a validated `Schema.Union` of `Schema.Struct`s over a structural `cause`. Each arm carries its human `reason`, and the `contract-skew-refused` arm also a typed `running`/`expected` version pair. Domain vocabulary stays in the domain, and the dependency arrow points out.

The framework's one field on that arm is `evidence`: `readonly { source: "local" | "remote"; line: string }[]`. It is a fixed type, not a third type parameter, because evidence is not domain volatility. Every transport that can fail produces output lines tagged with their source, and there is nothing for a domain to disagree about. `@kolu/surface-remote`'s session log already has this shape, so the producer hands its tail straight through.

## Why the fold envelope omits void

Every member's call folds to one wire shape: `{ mapKey, input }`, whatever the input is. The folded key rides outside `input`, so an entry input with its own `mapKey` field cannot collide with it.

A member that takes no input carries no `input` field at all: `{ mapKey }`, never `{ mapKey, input: undefined }`. The tempting version relies on two accidents: that JSON drops an `undefined` value, and that the decoder accepts the missing key. A schema library that rejects a missing declared-void field would break every void-input call the moment a lockfile drifted onto it.

Omitting the field makes "void = no input key" the one representation, so a dependency bump cannot silently break the wire.

## Why keys are codecs, not strings

A `Key` can be anything the schema validates: kolu's `HostKey` is a discriminated-sum object, not a string. But every channel name, dedup key and membership entry is keyed on a string. `KeyCodec<K>` bridges the two:

```ts
interface KeyCodec<K> { encode(key: K): string; decode(wire: string): K; }
```

An explicit codec keeps two boundaries apart. Parsing loose human input (`localhost`, `127.0.0.1`, `user@host`) is a separate function from decoding a canonical wire key, so a lax parse never leaks into the wire.

`encode` is a bare, cheap call because it sits on the hottest paths: a per-key cache lookup on every `entry(key)`, and a fold on every `entries` read. `decode` is paired with `keySchema.parse` and need not validate alone: the server re-validates the real key on arrival.

The typed end of a subscription follows the same discipline:

- A call to an absent key is not a thrown socket error. It is a typed rejection (`MAP_KEY_UNKNOWN`) for a procedure, and an immediate typed end for a stream.
- A key that leaves membership mid-stream ends its subscriptions with a typed `{ reason: "removed" }` before the session is torn down. A consumer sees a clean, expected end, never a socket-error frame after a typed end.

Absence is always a value you handle, never an exception that surprises you.

## Why the clock is per entry

A timestamp stamped on a remote host means nothing against the browser's wall clock, because the two clocks disagree. So `connected` carries the entry's measured `clockOffset`, and a host-stamped time is read through that entry's seam: `entry(key).clock.toLocal(remoteMs)`, which is `remoteMs − offset`.

The seam returns `number | null`. It returns `null`, never a silent identity, whenever the entry has no offset yet: warming, failed or not a member. The `null` is deliberately contagious, since `now − null` is a type error. A caller cannot fall back to the raw remote value, which is exactly the foreign-clock lie the seam prevents. This is [reactive honesty](/surface/reactive-honesty)'s rule, that a caught absence must surface, pushed into a type.

## Where it costs you

The map's wire makes one trade five times: it refuses a convenient shortcut in exchange for a lie it cannot tell.

| Choice | What you pay |
| --- | --- |
| No `absent` arm | You read status through a total lens instead of a nullable one. |
| A per-entry clock | You thread every host-stamped time through a seam, and render "—" while it warms. |
| A typed domain failure on `failed` | The app must say what each failure means, instead of showing one red dot. |

Each is more ceremony than a single-host client needs. The bet is that in a fleet, where the set changes underfoot and every fact belongs to one host, the ceremony is the only thing that stays true. If a future addition encodes an opinion about which host matters, that is policy dressed as framework, and it belongs in the app.
