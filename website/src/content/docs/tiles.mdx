A tile is a full terminal, not a preview. It carries the same **repo monogram**
as the dock, so you can see which repository it belongs to.

## A terminal the Conductor is driving

A thin border in the Conductor's colour around the terminal body means [the
Conductor](/conductor#who-is-typing) is driving that terminal. Nothing is added
to the title bar.

Reading works as usual: scroll, select, copy, search, open a link, and click
the terminal to focus it.

Typing is what is held back. Press a key, paste, or drop a file, and a card
appears over the terminal reading **The Conductor is driving this terminal**
with the role it plays. Nothing reaches the terminal.

The card offers **Take over** and **Dismiss**, and it also closes on
<kbd>Esc</kbd>, on a click elsewhere, or by itself after a few seconds.

Click **Take over** and the terminal is yours. The border changes, and a
**You're driving · Hand back** pill sits in the bottom left of the terminal
body. Click the pill to give it back. Both controls are on the terminal's row
of its [work](/work-card) in the Conductor too, on every work in progress, so
you can reach them without typing first.

Each move goes on the work's [timeline](/conductor#what-a-work-holds) and wakes
the Conductor, so the agent it directs is never typed at by two people at once.

The lock is kolu's state on the work rather than a flag in your browser. A
second device sees the same lock, and it survives a reload and a server
restart. It ends when the terminal leaves the work or the work ends. A terminal
the Conductor only watches is not locked, and neither are the `kolu` CLI, the
[MCP](/mcp) tools or the Conductor itself.

### Look from the Conductor

On a terminal's row of a work, or its card in the chat, in the
[Conductor](/conductor), **Look** opens a window over the chat with that
terminal live, in its own colours. A terminal kolu has no name for is called
by its host, such as **Implementor on nix@example**, never by an id.

- It never resizes the terminal. The window keeps one size whichever terminal
  is in it. A wider terminal scrolls sideways, or press **Fit width**.
- Each terminal of the same piece of work gets a tab.
- While the Conductor drives the terminal the window is read-only and offers
  **Take over**. After it you type in the window, and it offers **Hand back**.
  Typing while it is read-only raises the same card as on a tile.
- A terminal nobody drives takes your keys after a click.
- <kbd>Esc</kbd> closes the window, except while you are typing in the
  terminal. Then it goes to the terminal, and **×** or a click outside closes
  the window. **Open tile** goes to the terminal's own tile.

A [picked work](/work-card#the-picked-work) shows its terminals the same way,
in panes beside the chat rather than a window over it. A pane has the
window's head: who is driving, **Take over** or **Hand back**, **Fit width**
and **Open tile**. It never resizes the terminal either.

## Splits and tabs

Split a bottom pane with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>{"`"}</kbd>, and add tabs with
<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>{"`"}</kbd>. Every terminal opened under a
tile shows as a tab in that tile's bottom strip, including a split of a split,
and including one created from the shell with `kolu create --parent <id>`. In
the [Dock](/dock) the same terminals keep their tree.

A split someone else opens, such as an agent, expands the pane and takes the
focus, so you do not have to click it. If you are already working in another
split of that tile, you are left where you are.

To move a split, drag a Dock row's grip onto another row to make it a split of
that one, or onto its repo card's header to give it its own tile.

A split's shape is kept on the terminal, not in your browser: whether the
panel is collapsed, and where you dragged the divider. Reload the page, switch
hosts and come back, or open kolu on a second device, and the tile comes back
the way you left it. The program inside it is not resized on the way, because
the tile never lays itself out any way but the saved one.

## Restarting a terminal in place

The **↻** button on the title bar, or **Restart terminal** in the command
palette, gives a terminal a fresh shell on the same id: the same tile, folder
and canvas spot, with the split tree intact. Its splits restart with it, each on
its own id.

What comes back is what a new terminal would get, coding agents included. The
old scrollback goes with the old process.

If an agent is working or waiting for you, the first click only asks. Click
again within a few seconds to confirm. A plain shell restarts on the first
click, and the same confirmation guards the palette command.

For what the restart does to agents, see
[Restarting a terminal onto the current agents](/agents#restarting-a-terminal-onto-the-current-agents).

## Font zoom

Zoom the font with <kbd>Cmd</kbd>/<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>+</kbd> / <kbd>-</kbd>. It zooms
only the focused tile and is remembered per terminal.

## Rendering

- **WebGL rendering** with a canvas fallback, kept on your most recently used
  live tiles up to the browser's limit, so switching between them reloads nothing.
- **The way a tile is drawn never changes its size.** A tile's columns and rows
  come from the font's own measurement, not from the painter. A terminal that
  moves between WebGL and the plain renderer keeps the grid it had, so the
  program inside it is never resized by a change in how it is painted. Letters
  can still sit a fraction differently within a line.
- **Clickable URLs**. A long URL that wraps across lines survives a resize and
  opens whole.
- **Unicode 11** and **inline images** (sixel, iTerm2, kitty).

## Output and resizing

You never need a keypress or a reload to see output that already happened. A
dropped connection reconnects on its own, and a repaint queued while the
window was in the background runs when you come back. A terminal printing
faster than your link can carry costs you one screen, not the whole flood.

A pane whose output keeps coming back empty says so instead of sitting blank,
and kolu keeps retrying until output reaches it. A split you open later (a
collapsed panel, a background tab) stays empty until you reveal it, then
paints once at its real size.

Resize a tile and the pane states the size it wants. Dragging a divider,
zooming the font, or raising the soft keyboard publishes one size, once the
layout stops moving. The terminal changes size only when the machine running
your shell says its own screen did, so the pane and the shell are never laid
out differently — and a terminal is told a size only when its own box moved.

A pane states nothing until the tile around it knows its shape, so reloading
the page, coming back to a host, or opening a second kolu resizes nothing.
Opening a split takes its share of the tile once; collapsing it gives the
share back once.

Until a resize lands, about a quarter of a second later, the text keeps the
size it has, against the bottom left of the pane. A pane you made bigger shows
a blank band along the top.

Scroll up and the pane holds output rather than moving the text you are
reading. A resize waits with it: the terminal keeps the size it had until you
return to the bottom. The "new output" dot counts only output, so a resize
never lights it.

## Which tiles are live

Only the tiles you are looking at receive live output. On a phone that is the
tile on screen. On the desktop it is:

- the tile you have maximized;
- every tile in view on the canvas;
- the active tile, wherever it is, so your keys always reach the terminal you
  type into.

A tile panned off the canvas, a tile behind a maximized one, a background split
tab, and every tile while the [Conductor](/conductor) covers the canvas are left
alone. This is about the canvas, not the window: a kolu tab in the background of
your browser keeps its tiles live.

On the desktop, a tile that leaves your view keeps its feed for an hour. Come
back within the hour and nothing changed: you are where you were reading, with
no pause while the tile fetches a screen. The cost is the other side of that —
a tile you looked away from keeps receiving output for the whole hour. After
the hour it lets go, and coming back puts you at the end of its output.

Maximizing leaves the tiles behind it alone: they keep their own size, and
their programs are told nothing going in or coming back out. The tile you are
looking at is told the window's size when you maximize it, and its own size
again when you restore. Leaving maximized gives every tile then in view its
feed back at once.

A tile that is not live does not tell its terminal how big it is, so opening
kolu on a second device does not reshape every terminal on the host. Coming
back to a pane keeps the old screen up until the current one arrives, and the
activity dot tells you a terminal changed while you were elsewhere.

## A tip under the title bar

In a terminal with [coding agents](/agents), a tile sometimes shows a bar
between its title bar and the terminal, suggesting what to do next there. The
bar names where the suggestion comes from.

| When | The tip | The buttons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You are at a shell inside a repository | Launch an agent: one button for each agent in the terminal's set, in the set's order (for example `claude` `codex` `opencode` `pi` `omp`) | Click the one you want: it types that agent's name and presses Enter. If they do not all fit, the last button is **+N**, which opens a menu of the rest |
| You launch one of your agents and it waits for your first message | Try a skill: one of kolu's own, with a line on what it does | Puts `/kolu ` into the agent's input and leaves the cursor after it, so you finish the message and send it yourself |

Only the buttons do anything. Clicking the rest of the bar does not select or
move the tile.

A tip shows only on the active tile, on the desktop layout, on a running
terminal. The skill tip appears as soon as the agent you launched is running,
before it has answered anything. The launch tip needs the shell prompt in front,
so running `ls`, `ssh` or `vim` clears it until the prompt is back.

The bar slides open while its suggestion applies, and folds away when it no
longer does: you start an agent, send it that first message, or leave it. While
the bar is open the terminal below has a couple fewer rows.

### Folding the tip to a tab

To keep the terminal's full height, click the small arrow at the right end of
the bar. The bar folds into a **tab** hanging from the bottom right of the title
bar, showing only the first words ("Launch an agent", "Try a skill"). Hover it
to read the whole tip, and click it to open the bar again. **Toggle tip bar** in
the command palette does the same.

The choice is one setting for every tile, and kolu remembers it.

## On touch devices

A **mobile key bar** sends the keys soft keyboards lack: Esc, Tab, arrows and
<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>C</kbd>, with two sticky modifiers you tap to arm. The full story is in
[kolu on a Phone](/mobile).
