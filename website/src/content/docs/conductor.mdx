The **Conductor** is one chat with an agent that runs the agents in your terminals. You say what you want done. It opens a piece of work, creates terminals on whichever host that work belongs on, briefs the agents in them, and answers their questions or brings them to you.

<Aside type="caution" title="alpha">
  The Conductor is alpha, and its shape is still changing. Upgrading kolu can leave you with a new, empty conversation: the old transcript is not carried over. Keep anything you need outside it, in the repository or in a file.
</Aside>

## What it is

The Conductor is a conversation with Claude, run by kolu-server (the kolu process your browser talks to) in the `$HOME` of its machine. Its tools are kolu's own [MCP tools](/mcp), across every host: it opens terminals, speaks to their agents, reads their screens and closes them.

A terminal's name includes its host, such as `local/3f29…` or `remote:nix@prod/9a1b…`. Add a host mid-conversation and its terminals appear. Remove one and its terminals report themselves gone.

There is one Conductor, on the machine running kolu-server. While that machine is off, nothing supervises your other hosts.

## Open it

<Steps>

1. Click the **Conductor** tab, the first tab in the chrome bar, marked `◆`. Or press <kbd>Cmd</kbd>/<kbd>Super</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>O</kbd>.
2. Type what you want done, and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. For example: `waterfall fix the login error in ~/code/app`.
3. To get back, click a host tab, or press <kbd>Cmd</kbd>/<kbd>Super</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>O</kbd> again to return to the host you came from.

</Steps>

On a phone, the Conductor is the first entry in the pull-down chrome sheet.

Leaving the Conductor does not close it. When you come back, everything is as you left it, including a half-typed message.

To ask about one terminal, use its tile action **Ask the Conductor about this**.

## Type a message

| You type | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| text, then <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Sends the message. |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Starts a new line. |
| `@` | Completes a terminal from any host into a chip. The agent receives its `host/id` and intent. |
| `/` | Completes one of the agent's own commands. |
| <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Steers the running turn (see below). |

When the Conductor puts a choice to you, it arrives as a form: one button per option and a box for your own answer.

## Talk to it while it works

The message box never locks. While a turn is running:

- <kbd>Enter</kbd> queues your message at the agent. It shows as _queued_ and runs when the turn is over. **Stop** cancels the turn, and queued messages still run next.
- <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd> steers: your words go into the running turn, and the Conductor reads them now. The status line shows **↯ Alt+⏎ steers** when you can.

Steering is offered only when the agent supports it. A steer the agent does not accept is sent as a queued message, so your words are never lost. A message with attachments is always queued. Once one message has been queued in a session, every later one is queued too, until you start a new session.

## The status line

The line above the message box says what the Conductor is doing:

| Part | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| state | **working** (on what, and for how long), **asking you**, or **idle** |
| **N pieces of work · M waiting on you** | what it has in flight, and how many of those are stopped on you. Hidden at zero. On a narrow window, click it to open the work column. |
| waiting | how many wakes are held until the turn ends |
| **↯ Alt+⏎ steers** | shown while you can steer |

A **piece of work** is one kolu is storing and has not finished, so a waterfall on three terminals counts once. **M waiting on you** counts those stopped on you.

## What wakes it

The Conductor does not sit waiting on a terminal. It says what it has set going and ends its turn. kolu starts its next turn when one of these happens in a member terminal:

| Wake | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| `ready` | An agent has started and has not been prompted yet. Also sent for an idle agent the Conductor has never spoken to, such as a resumed session. See [how kolu knows](/agent-detection#before-the-first-prompt). |
| `stopped` | An agent the Conductor has spoken to finished its turn. |
| `asking` | The agent stopped to ask a question. |
| `gone` | The terminal was closed, or its host left. |
| `asleep` | The terminal was put to sleep. Sent once; the terminal stays in the work. |
| `exited` | The agent quit or crashed, and the terminal is back at its shell prompt. |
| `ended` | A `run` command finished and no agent ever appeared. |
| `taken-over` | You took the terminal over. |
| `handed-back` | You gave it back. |

Nothing wakes the Conductor just because time passed, and a terminal that belongs to no piece of work never wakes it.

An agent can take seconds or minutes to start. So the Conductor creates a terminal, ends its turn, and sends the brief when the terminal reports `ready`.

A wake shows in the chat as a line starting with **kolu**. Anything that happens during a turn is held, newest state per terminal, and arrives as one wake when the turn ends. Your own messages are never held.

What wakes cannot tell it:

- A command that fails before anything starts, such as `omp: command not found`, wakes nothing.
- No wake says whether a program succeeded. The Conductor reads the screen for that.
- If kolu cannot deliver a wake (the agent will not start, or you are not signed in), it keeps the wake and posts one notice saying why. It tries again when the conversation next goes idle.

## What it is working on

kolu stores a **piece of work**: a thing with a name, not a guess about which terminals look related. The Conductor opens one with `work_open`, naming its title, repo and branch, and every terminal joins with a **role**: planner, implementor, or other. A create or adopt call is refused without both.

A terminal it creates is a member at once, and it adopts one you point it at. It drops a terminal whose part is done, and one closed any other way leaves with `gone`. The tools are on [the MCP page](/mcp#the-conductors-pieces-of-work).

The `works` resource reads them back, so a fresh conversation picks up work in flight.

### What a work holds

| Part | What is in it |
| --- | --- |
| Timeline | Every event kolu saw, append-only, each naming who it was: you, an agent, CI or kolu. An agent coming up, stopping or asking, a `.waterfall/` file, the PR, its checks, the merge. |
| Decisions | Every question put to you about this work: the options, what you picked, and whether you answered from the card or the chat. |
| Setup | The host, the mode, the harness and model each role runs on, and what to do when CI goes green. |
| Members | Each terminal by `host/id`, its role, the agent kolu last saw in it, and who is driving it. |
| Outcome | **merged**, **closed**, or **abandoned** when every member left and nothing landed. The `.waterfall/` files are copied into kolu as it ends. |

A work holds its latest **30** agent state changes; older ones fold into one line reading **N earlier agent state changes**. Nothing else folds. kolu keeps the **last 20** finished works with their files.

### Who is typing

While a work is in progress, each member terminal is read-only to you until you press **Take over** on its tile. Taking over, and handing back, each write a line on the work's timeline and wake the Conductor: it is told to leave that terminal alone, then to read its screen before using it again.

Both work while the agent is down or mid-turn. The lock lives in the stored work, so every device sees it, and it ends when the terminal leaves the work. [Tiles](/tiles#a-terminal-the-conductor-is-driving) has the rest.

### The work card

On a wide window the works stand in a column beside the chat. On a narrow window, or a phone, the status line's **N pieces of work** opens the same column above it.

Each work is one card: its title and how long it has run, **repo · host · mode**, `PR #n`, where it stands, and a time bar split by who it has waited on. Violet is you, amber is CI, teal is the agents.

Press the head and it opens:

| Part | What you get |
| --- | --- |
| The question waiting for you | The same form the chat shows. Answering it here is answering it there. |
| Decisions | Question and answer, newest last, each saying where the answer came from. |
| Timeline | Every event with its time, and how long that step took. |
| Terminals | By role: how that agent stands, **Look** for its last lines, and the name to go to its tile. |
| Files | The worktree's `.waterfall/` files and the copies kolu kept, rendered in place by the [Code tab](/code-tab)'s viewer. |
| Setup | Host, mode, the harness and model per role, and what to do on green. |
| Pull request | Its number and title, the checks, and a link. |

Below them, **Finished** stands collapsed, each with its outcome, its PR and the files kolu kept. A conversation with no work shows no count, and the column reads **Nothing in progress.**

## Read the conversation

The header shows who the agent is signed in as, the **model**, the context used (as `148k/1M`), and the **⋯** menu. Click the model to change it, or the reasoning effort and permission mode. The model you choose survives a restart.

| ⋯ menu item | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **New session** | Starts an empty conversation. The messages and their attached files are deleted. The pieces of work stay, because a work is kolu's rather than the conversation's. It asks first, and there is no undo. |
| **Sign in…** | Shown while the agent is not signed in. |
| **Copy transcript** | Copies the whole conversation as plain text. |
| **How to use** | Opens the help. On a wide window the help is a column beside the chat. |
| tool servers | `kolu ✓` when kolu's tools are attached, or the agent's reason when they are not. |

In the transcript:

- Tool calls between two messages fold into one **Details** line with a count. Running calls, failed calls, questions and your messages always stay outside it.
- A card for a terminal the Conductor created shows the agent's state, **Look**, and **open tile**, which takes you to the terminal.
- **Look** shows the last 40 lines of the screen, refreshed about every two seconds while you can see it.
- A `— new session —` line means kolu could not resume the conversation after a restart. The agent remembers nothing above it.

## When it asks you

A question from the Conductor, or a permission its agent needs, appears as a form in the chat. Nothing times out. Dismissing the form is an answer too: the agent is told you declined.

The badge on the Conductor tab is how many things are waiting on you: a work stopped on a question or a decision of yours, plus any question asked outside a work. It is violet, and absent at zero. A question chimes, notifies and counts toward the app badge like any [needs-you](/notifications) source, unless the conversation is on screen in a focused window. A turn simply ending makes no sound.

## Attach a file

Paste an image into the message box, or drop a file onto the chat. A chip shows what is attached, and you can remove it before sending.

| File | What the agent gets |
| --- | --- |
| an image | the image |
| a text file | its text |
| anything else | a link to where kolu saved it |

There are two limits: 10 MiB per file and 20 MiB per message. Over either, the message box tells you before anything is sent. An agent that does not accept a kind of file is refused out loud.

## Sign in

If the agent is not signed in, the Conductor cannot run a turn. It shows the agent's own ways to sign in.

For Claude, kolu runs Claude's sign-in command, shows its links, and gives you a line to type the code into. The message that was refused is sent again once you are signed in. There is no box for an API key.

## Run a waterfall

A **waterfall** takes a change from request to merged PR. Ask for one, or ask the Conductor to build something end to end. It has two modes, chosen per waterfall:

| Mode | What runs |
| --- | --- |
| Full | A planner writes a plan, you approve it, an implementor builds it, and the planner reviews the PR. |
| Trivial | One implementor. No plan, approval, review or brief file. |

<Steps>

1. **Setup.** Every time, it asks which host, which agent CLI (the harness) and model run each agent, what to do when CI goes green, and the mode. It skips what your request already says. The questions travel in the call that opens the work, so they arrive as one form.
2. **Plan** (full only). A planner opens in a fresh worktree, writes `.waterfall/plan.md`, and stops.
3. **Approve** (full only). The Conductor shows you a summary. You approve, or say what to change.
4. **Implement.** The implementor opens with the brief in `.waterfall/brief.md` (full), or with your request (trivial). It stops when its PR is open. A doubt it stops on is answered from the plan, the request or the planner; only a decision that is yours comes to you.
5. **Review** (full only). The planner reviews the PR and the implementor addresses it, until the planner is satisfied. Something you ask for mid-round waits for the next round.
6. **Finish.** The implementor makes a final refactor pass, then runs CI the way the repo says to run it, fixing it until it is green.
7. **On green.** The Conductor does what the work's recorded decision says: it merges the PR, or hands it to you.
8. **Close up.** Once the PR is merged or closed, the Conductor closes the terminals it opened for it.

</Steps>

A path does not choose a host: `~/code/odu` can exist on several machines. So the Conductor always asks which host before it creates a terminal.

The Conductor never implements anything itself, never starts an agent or opens a PR without your approval, and never closes an implementor before its PR is merged or closed. Its standing instructions are at the top of every new session's transcript.

## After a restart

kolu-server restarts when it is upgraded or its service restarts. The conversation comes back with its transcript and its model, and every piece of work comes back too, because a work is stored in its own right rather than inside the conversation.

An agent that finished while kolu-server was down still wakes the Conductor, and nothing wakes it twice. A running turn and held wakes are lost. While the Conductor is alpha, an upgrade can still start an empty conversation instead (see the note at the top).

### What a backup of kolu holds

kolu's [rotated snapshots](/troubleshooting#kolu-lost-my-session-or-my-settings-look-wrong) include the pieces of work, so restoring the server store puts back your preferences, your host pool and the works at once, live. Two things are not in them: the conversation, whose other half lives in the agent's own session store, and the files a finished work kept, which are copies on disk.
