kolu runs on phones and tablets: the same workspace, re-shaped for a thumb. The
viewport size picks the drawer layout, and the input type picks touch-sized hit
targets (at least 24px).

The two are independent. kolu reads the pointer, so a narrow desktop browser
still gets the desktop canvas.

## On a phone

- **One tile at a time**, fullscreen. You swipe between terminals instead of
  moving around a canvas.
- A two-row **soft-key bar** sends the keys touch keyboards lack: Esc, Tab, the
  arrows, Ctrl-C, `/` and Enter. Two sticky modifiers stay armed until you use
  them. The keys reach whichever terminal or split has focus.
- When the tile you are on is a terminal [the Conductor is
  driving](/tiles#a-terminal-the-conductor-is-driving), a border in the
  Conductor's colour runs round it. Tap a key and the key bar sends nothing:
  a card appears over the terminal with **Take over** on it.
- A **bottom-drawer** inspector takes the place of the desktop right panel. Pull
  the chrome down from the top for app controls.
- Only the tile you are looking at is live. The other terminals on the host keep
  running, but the phone does not stream their output and does not tell them how
  big its screen is. So opening kolu on a phone reshapes nothing else on that
  machine, and the soft keyboard only affects the terminal you type in.
- Swipe to another tile and you see the screen you left, replaced as soon as the
  current one arrives. The activity dot tells you a tile changed while you were
  elsewhere.

## On a tablet or an unfolded foldable

A wide touch screen (an iPad, an Android tablet, a Galaxy Z Fold unfolded) gets
a **two-pane** layout: a terminal rail down one side, the active terminal
filling the rest, with the same touch-sized controls as the phone. A real mouse
still gets the canvas.

Folding or unfolding the screen keeps the same terminal view, and so does
switching between the touch and desktop layouts.

## Switch machines from the phone

The pull-down chrome sheet carries a **host row**, one chip per machine, driving
the same [multi-host](/remote-hosts) engine as the desktop. Each chip shows the
machine's name (**Home** plus the hostname for local, the ssh target for a
remote), a connection pip reading the whole chain as the
[desktop host tabs](/remote-hosts) do, and that machine's attention counts:
active, needs you, unread.

Tap a chip to switch, with no reload. Tap **+** to open an add-machine section
inside the sheet. The chrome stays above the canvas in every state, so a host
that is still connecting or unreachable can never leave you without a way back
to the row. The row follows the order you dragged the desktop host tabs into,
which is saved on the server; the drag itself is desktop-only.

## The Conductor on a phone

[**`◆ Conductor`**](/conductor) is the first entry in the pull-down sheet, above
the host chips. Tap it and the conversation takes the whole screen; tap a host
chip to go back to that machine.

A phone that opens kolu in a new tab lands on the Conductor. A reload stays
where you were, and a link or a notification to a terminal still opens that
terminal. A tablet opens on its host.

Its badge is how many things need you, in the needs-you violet, and absent
when the answer is none. The same count rides the pull handle itself, so you
read it without pulling the sheet down.

A phone is too narrow to stand the [pieces of work](/work-card) beside the
conversation, so they are two tabs under it, **Work** and **Chat**. A new tab
opens on **Chat**, and a reload stays on the one you were on.

The **Work** tab shows how many works need you, in the needs-you violet. The
status line's **N pieces of work · M need you** opens it, and so does tapping a
work chip in the chat, at that work, a finished one included. A work you tap
is a page of its own, with **← All work** above it and the tabs still under it.

On a phone, **Look** fills the screen. The terminal keeps its own size, so you
pan sideways or tap **Fit width**. After **Take over** the key bar appears
under it. A work's page shows its terminals the same way, one at a time with a
tab each, and the key bar under the one you took over.
